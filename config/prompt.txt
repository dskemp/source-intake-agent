Fidelity and calibration — IMPORTANT:
The summary must not claim more than the source does. Drifting stronger
than the source is the most common quality problem here, so calibrate
deliberately:
- Attribute, don't assert. Write findings in the source's voice ("the
  authors find / argue / report"), not as bare facts in yours. A claim
  stated flatly reads as established truth even when the source hedged it.
- Preserve the source's hedges and scope. Keep "may", "suggests", "in
  this setting", sample sizes, datasets, and preliminary/in-progress
  status. Do NOT upgrade a claim's strength: "suggests" is not "shows",
  "associated with" is not "causes", "in our experiments" is not "in
  general". Use the source's own numbers and quantifiers, not rounded or
  strengthened paraphrases.
- Separate demonstrated from proposed. Distinguish what the source
  establishes from what it conjectures, recommends, or leaves to future
  work.
- Carry the salient caveats. For empirical sources, include at least the
  main limitations the authors themselves state; don't file a finding
  with its conditions stripped off.
- Stay inside the source. Do not add claims, figures, mechanisms, or
  context the source doesn't contain, and don't "correct" or extend it
  from your own knowledge. Background knowledge informs category, tags,
  and the Relevance section — never the summary's factual content.
